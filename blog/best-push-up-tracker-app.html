How to Choose a Push-Up Tracker App

By PushCheck · Published May 24, 2026 · Updated October 8, 2026

A push-up tracker should fit the way you want to use it. You might want a camera to count reps, a record of your daily totals, or a shared board for a challenge with friends. Start with that choice before comparing features.

This is PushCheck's guide to choosing a tracker. We make an app for manual push-up logging and group accountability. We have not tested every tracker, and there is no single best app for everyone.

Decide how you want to log your reps

PushCheck uses manual check-ins. Finish your push-ups, open the app, and enter your count. It does not automatically count reps or judge your form. If automatic counting is your priority, look for an app that specifically supports it.

Manual entry also means a number on the board is a member's check-in. Optional proof photos add context, but a photo is not verification of every rep. Agree with your friends on what you will count before the challenge starts.

Look for the board you will actually use

  • Today's Board: see group members' logged counts and check-in times.
  • Total rankings: compare accumulated reps.
  • Streak rankings: compare consistency instead of only volume.
  • Personal stats: review your current streak, best streak, daily average, and all-time totals.

Those views answer different questions. Today's Board tells you who has checked in. A total shows accumulated work. A streak gives consistency its own place. Pick the comparison your group wants to follow.

Try the basic routine before adding rules

  1. Start solo or create a private group.
  2. Set a daily goal. If you are creating a group, agree on the goal together.
  3. Share the invite code with your friends.
  4. Log your completed reps and check the board.
  5. After the first week, discuss whether the goal and group routine still fit.

A one-week review is a suggestion for your group, not an automatic app program. PushCheck tracks your check-ins; it does not prescribe a training plan.

Know how corrections work

If you did the work but forgot the check-in, you can add or edit your own entries from the past seven days. Group creators can correct members' check-ins up to 30 days back. These are logging corrections, not a reason to record reps you did not do.

The optional shame timer sends a nightly reminder about members who have not reached their goal. Decide as a group whether that kind of reminder is useful. The app also supports solo tracking if you want to begin on your own.

Check the price of the features you need

PushCheck's core tracking, private groups, check-ins, streaks, and leaderboards are free. PushCheck Pro is an optional one-time unlock for public groups and challenge seasons. There are no ads or recurring subscriptions.

A private friend challenge can use the free core features. If you want a start and end date with standings for that specific period, look at Pro's challenge seasons. See the FAQ for the current feature and pricing details.

Choose based on the job

For PushCheck, the job is straightforward: set a goal, log your push-ups, and see who checked in. Choose it if that is the routine you want. For a practical group setup, read how to run a push-up challenge with friends.

Start with one check-in

Track on your own or invite friends to a private group. Core features are free; public groups and challenge seasons are optional Pro features.

How to Run a Push-Up Challenge with Friends

By PushCheck · Published May 24, 2026 · Updated October 8, 2026

A group chat is a good place to start a push-up challenge. A shared board gives the daily check-ins somewhere to live. With PushCheck, you set a goal, share an invite code, and enter your reps so the group can see them.

This guide explains the setup and the decisions to make together. An app cannot promise that everyone will finish. It can make the activity you log visible.

1. Agree on the challenge before creating it

Decide who is joining, what daily goal you want, and what you will count as a completed rep. The goal belongs to your group. You do not have to copy a large daily number from an internet challenge.

Agree on the comparison too. Are you following total reps, daily check-ins, or streaks? PushCheck has different views for these, so choose the one that matches the challenge you actually want.

2. Create a private group and share the code

  1. Download PushCheck on iPhone and create your account.
  2. Create a group and set its daily push-up goal.
  3. Share the group's invite code with the friends joining you.
  4. Log your completed reps and open Today's Board.

The group creator manages the settings and membership. Private groups are part of the free core app. If your friends are not ready yet, you can track solo and invite them later.

3. Use the board for the question you are asking

Today's Board shows logged counts and check-in times. It is the place to see who has entered their work today.

Total and Streak rankings show different kinds of progress. Total reps give you accumulated volume. Streak rankings give consistency a separate comparison. Do not treat them as the same contest.

Reps are entered manually. Optional proof photos let members attach a photo to a check-in; they do not automatically count or validate the reps. Keep your group's expectations clear.

4. Choose how you want reminders to feel

PushCheck includes an optional nightly shame timer that calls out members who have not hit their goal. Discuss it before switching it on. Some groups want that reminder; others prefer to check the board themselves.

Use your existing group chat to discuss the challenge, and check PushCheck for the logged counts and standings.

5. Correct missed logs honestly

Members can add or edit their own check-ins from the past seven days. Group creators can edit members' check-ins up to 30 days back. If someone did the push-ups but forgot to enter them, these windows let you correct the record.

At the end of your first week, review the setup together. Is the goal clear? Is everyone able to find the board? Are people using the same logging rules? Fix the confusing part before adding another rule.

Running a challenge with a finish date

You can agree on a finish date in your group chat while using free private-group tracking. For an in-app date window and separate season standings, the group creator needs PushCheck Pro to set up a challenge season.

Seasons rank the reps recorded within the selected window. The creator can generate a results card when the season ends. Keep season standings separate from the all-time totals when discussing the result.

Pro is an optional one-time unlock for public groups and challenge seasons. Core private groups, check-ins, streaks, and leaderboards stay free. There are no ads or recurring subscriptions. The FAQ covers the current details.

Keep the first challenge simple

Start with a goal, a group, and a clear way to log the work. You can add a season or join public challenges later. If you are still comparing tracking methods, read how to choose a push-up tracker.
